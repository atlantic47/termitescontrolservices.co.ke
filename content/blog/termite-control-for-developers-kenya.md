# Termite Control for Developers in Kenya: Site-Wide Protection for Every Project

Real estate development in Kenya is one of the most competitive and capital-intensive business sectors in the economy. Whether you are building ten townhouses in Syokimau, fifty apartments in Ngara, or a mixed-use complex in Westlands, every unit you deliver represents a significant investment — and every unit you deliver without termite protection is a latent liability that can come back to you in the form of buyer complaints, warranty claims, and reputational damage.

Termite control for developers is a specialized discipline that goes beyond treating a single house. It requires site-wide planning, multi-stage coordination with construction programmes, bulk documentation for lending institutions, and an understanding of how to cost termite treatment efficiently at scale. Pestraid Kenya has worked with dozens of Kenyan developers and has developed a turnkey termite management programme specifically for development projects.

## Why Termite Protection Is a Developer's Responsibility in Kenya

### Legal and Regulatory Obligations

The National Building Code of Kenya requires anti-termite treatment during construction for buildings in termite-prone areas. Most county building inspectors in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, and Nakuru now require evidence of treatment as part of the occupation certificate process. Non-compliance can result in certificates being withheld, delaying sales or occupancy.

### Bank and Mortgage Requirements Affecting Your Buyers

When your buyers seek mortgage financing, their banks will require a **Termite Treatment Certificate** for the property before final loan disbursement. Without this certificate, your buyers cannot complete their purchase — directly impacting your cash flow and project timeline. A delayed certificate for 50 units across a development can cost a developer millions of shillings in delayed receipts.

Pestraid Kenya issues bulk treatment certificates in the format required by all major Kenyan banks, processed efficiently to align with your sales schedule.

### Warranty and Defects Liability Period

Most Kenyan property sale agreements include a defects liability period of 6–24 months. During this period, buyer complaints about termite damage can constitute a valid claim against the developer. A properly documented termite treatment programme — with chemical records, application dates, and warranty letters — provides your legal defence if such claims arise.

### Reputational Risk in a Competitive Market

Nairobi's property market is increasingly sophisticated. Buyers — especially those who have invested in properties that were later damaged by termites — actively ask whether a building has a valid treatment warranty before committing. A development with documented, warranted termite protection is a marketable differentiator in a crowded market.

## Pestraid Kenya's Developer Programme: What Is Included

### 1. Pre-Project Site Assessment

Before ground is broken on a new development, Pestraid Kenya carries out a site-wide termite survey. This covers:
- Identification of existing termite colonies within the site boundaries (in trees, stumps, buried timber, and soil)
- Soil profile assessment to determine optimum treatment rates and expected barrier longevity
- Review of the site plan and architectural drawings to identify all treatment zones

### 2. Termite Management Plan (TMP)

For developments of five or more units, Pestraid Kenya produces a formal **Termite Management Plan** covering:
- Treatment specifications for all buildings on the site
- A phased treatment schedule aligned to the construction programme
- Chemical product data sheets and application rate schedules
- A quality assurance protocol including inspection checkpoints at each treatment stage

This document is submitted to the project QS, architect, and main contractor as part of the project specification.

### 3. Multi-Stage Treatment Execution

Pestraid Kenya's treatment team coordinates with the site foreman at each critical stage:

**Stage 1:** Foundation excavation and backfill treatment for each building block as construction progresses

**Stage 2:** Sub-slab formation level treatment for each unit before blinding is poured — phased across the site as construction progresses unit by unit or block by block

**Stage 3:** Service penetration treatment and sealing for all plumbing, drainage, and electrical penetrations

**Stage 4:** External perimeter treatment for completed buildings before landscaping and external works are finished

### 4. Bulk Documentation and Certification

For each unit on the site, Pestraid Kenya issues:
- An individual unit treatment certificate in the buyer's name (for mortgage applications)
- A site-wide treatment summary record for the developer's compliance file
- A warranty letter covering each unit for the specified warranty period

All documentation is formatted for immediate submission to Kenyan banks and county councils.

### 5. Ongoing Maintenance and Re-inspection Programme

Pestraid Kenya offers developers an ongoing site management contract for post-handover inspections and warranty maintenance across all sold units. This service can be offered to buyers as an added-value aftercare package — a marketable feature that distinguishes your development.

## Case Study: What Happens Without Developer Termite Control

A Nairobi developer completed a 20-unit townhouse project in 2021. No termite treatment was applied during construction. By 2023, buyers in six units had reported termite activity in door frames, built-in wardrobes, and skirting boards. The developer faced:

- Post-construction treatment costs for all 20 units: approximately KES 500,000
- Timber replacement costs claimed by buyers: approximately KES 180,000
- Legal costs associated with buyer complaints: KES 75,000
- Delayed resale of remaining units while treatment was completed: estimated revenue impact of KES 800,000

Total avoidable cost: approximately KES 1,555,000 — against a treatment cost during construction of approximately KES 160,000 (KES 8,000 per unit × 20 units). The return on investment for correct termite control during construction was unmistakable.
