# Termite Control for New Buildings in Kenya: Build Smart, Build Protected

Every new building constructed in Kenya is an investment of hundreds of thousands - or millions - of shillings. Yet a significant proportion of those buildings are completed without any termite control programme in place, leaving them vulnerable to the silent, continuous destruction that subterranean termites cause from the moment the building is occupied.

Termite control for new buildings is not simply a precaution. In many Kenyan counties and for most bank-financed construction projects, it is a legal and financial requirement. This guide explains what is required, when it must be done, how it works, and how to get it right from the first day of ground-breaking.

## Why New Buildings Need Termite Control Before Occupation

A common misconception among Kenyan property owners is that termite problems develop slowly and only affect old buildings. In reality, a new building can be colonized very quickly:

- **Construction disturbance** disturbs soil, exposing termite galleries and stimulating foraging activity. The cleared ground, loose soil, and stored timber on a construction site are ideal foraging targets.
- **New timber installations** - door frames, window frames, roof trusses, floor joists - provide the exact food source termites seek.
- **Soil settlement** around foundations creates new pathways for termites to exploit.
- **Moisture from curing concrete** and early plumbing installation attracts foraging workers.

Buildings in active construction zones or on previously undeveloped land are at particularly high risk because the construction itself disturbs resident termite colonies, stimulating aggressive new foraging.

## The Kenyan Regulatory Framework for Termite Control in New Buildings

### National Building Code of Kenya

The National Building Code specifies that buildings in termite-risk areas must include an anti-termite treatment programme during construction. While enforcement varies by county, most county government building inspection departments in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, and Nakuru require evidence of termite treatment as part of the certificate of occupation process.

### Bank and Mortgage Requirements

Most Kenyan banks and mortgage providers - including Kenya Commercial Bank (KCB), Housing Finance Company (HFC), Stanbic, and Absa - require a **Termite Treatment Certificate** before disbursing the final tranche of construction finance or approving a mortgage on a new property. This certificate must be issued by a PCPB-licensed pest control operator and include the product used, application date, area covered, and warranty details.

## The Complete Termite Control Programme for a New Building

A properly executed termite control programme for a new building in Kenya consists of multiple treatment stages aligned with the construction programme:

### Stage 1: Site Survey and Pre-Treatment Colony Assessment

Before construction begins, Pestraid Kenya carries out a site survey to:
- Identify any existing termite colonies within the building footprint or in surrounding trees and stumps
- Assess the soil type and profile (this determines treatment rates and expected longevity)
- Review the architectural drawings to identify all critical treatment zones

Any existing colonies within the site are eliminated before the chemical barrier is laid.

### Stage 2: Foundation Excavation and Backfill Treatment

As detailed in [Pre-Construction Termite Treatment](/blog/pre-construction-termite-treatment-kenya):
- The base and sides of foundation trenches are treated with diluted termiticide immediately after excavation
- Each layer of backfill around the foundation footings receives a measured termiticide application
- This creates a vertical treated zone from the foundation footing up to the formation level

### Stage 3: Sub-Slab Formation Level Treatment

This is the single most important stage. Immediately before the blinding concrete is poured under the ground floor slab, Pestraid Kenya applies termiticide uniformly across the entire formation level at the specified label rate. Full details in [Termite Treatment Before the Slab](/blog/termite-treatment-before-slab).

### Stage 4: Service Penetration Treatment and Sealing

Every plumbing pipe, drainage sleeve, electrical conduit, and cable penetration through the slab is individually treated and sealed, eliminating the most common entry points termites exploit in treated buildings.

### Stage 5: External Perimeter Treatment After Construction

Once the building structure is complete and the external perimeter is accessible, a supplementary external perimeter treatment may be applied around the finished structure. This is particularly important where:
- Landscaping has introduced new soil that may carry termites
- Construction extended over a long period during which soil may have been disturbed
- The building has any hard-standing areas that could not be treated earlier

### Stage 6: Certification and Documentation

Pestraid Kenya issues:
- A full pre-construction treatment certificate with all required information for banks and county councils
- Application records with product batch numbers and dilution rates
- A warranty covering the treatment period
- A schedule for follow-up inspection visits

## New Building Termite Control for Different Construction Types

### Standard Concrete Slab Construction

The most common construction method in Kenya. All five stages described above apply. The sub-slab formation level treatment is the most critical stage.

### Timber-Frame Buildings

Where the primary structural system is timber-framed (common in coastal Kenya and rural areas), termite control is even more critical. In addition to soil treatment, all structural timber should be treated with a preservative (borate or copper-chrome-arsenate based) before installation. Read our guide on [Termite Treatment for Timber Structures](/blog/termite-treatment-timber-structures) for specific guidance.

### Multi-Storey Apartment Buildings

For multi-storey developments, termite treatment focuses on the ground floor and basement levels - the zones in contact with or closest to the soil. Upper floors are not at direct soil contact risk but benefit from treatment of shared service cores and basement levels.

Pestraid Kenya produces site-wide termite management plans for multi-storey developments.

See [Termite Control for Developers in Kenya](/blog/termite-control-for-developers-kenya).

### Buildings with Basements

Basement construction introduces additional treatment complexity. The full perimeter of basement excavations, the basement floor slab formation level, and the external faces of basement walls all require treatment before waterproofing and backfilling.

## Coordinating Termite Control Into Your Construction Programme

Poor coordination is the most common reason new buildings miss the critical treatment stages. The key points to plan:

**Appoint Pestraid Kenya before ground is broken.** We assess the site, provide a stage-by-stage treatment schedule aligned to your construction programme, and communicate directly with your site foreman.

**Include treatment as a BoQ (Bill of Quantities) line item.** This ensures the cost is budgeted, the contractor is contractually responsible for coordination, and the treatment is not skipped to save time.

**Ensure the site foreman has Pestraid Kenya's contact number.** The sub-slab treatment must happen at the specific moment between formation compaction and blinding pour - the foreman must call us in advance so we can be on site at the right time.

**Do not stockpile timber on untreated ground.** Any timber stored on site for extended periods before installation should be kept elevated off the ground and away from soil.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if I missed the pre-construction stage?**
[Post-construction termite treatment](/blog/post-construction-termite-treatment-kenya) is available for buildings that were completed without treatment. It is more expensive and less complete in coverage, but it delivers effective protection. It is never too late to treat.

**Do I still need termite control if I am using concrete block walls?**
Yes. Concrete does not repel termites. They exploit hairline cracks, expansion joints, and service penetrations to bypass concrete and reach timber elements. No material used in modern Kenyan construction is termite-proof without a chemical soil barrier.

**Can the builder apply the termite treatment themselves?**
No. Treatment must be applied by a PCPB-licensed pest control operator using registered products. Treatment applied by unlicensed individuals will not be accepted for a bank certificate and may not use the correct products or application rates.

**How do I book treatment that fits my construction schedule?**
