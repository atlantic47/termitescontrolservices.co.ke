# Termite Inspection Report in Kenya: What It Contains, Who Needs It, and How to Get One

A termite inspection report is one of the most important documents a Kenyan property owner, buyer, or developer can hold. It is a formal written record of a professional examination of a property for termite activity, damage, and risk — and it has significant practical value for property transactions, mortgage applications, insurance, and property management.

Yet many Kenyans are unclear about what a termite inspection report actually contains, when it is legally or financially required, how it differs from a treatment certificate, and what credentials the inspector must hold for the report to be accepted by banks, insurers, and county councils. This guide answers all of these questions comprehensively.

## What Is a Termite Inspection Report?

A termite inspection report (sometimes called a Wood Destroying Organism Report, Timber Pest Inspection Report, or Pest Inspection Certificate) is a formal written document produced by a licensed pest control professional after a systematic, physical inspection of a property. It records:

1. **The scope of the inspection** — which areas of the property were inspected and which were inaccessible
2. **Evidence of active termite infestation** — confirmed presence of live termites, active mud tubes, or active feeding damage
3. **Evidence of past termite activity** — historic damage, old mud tubes, repaired timber
4. **Conditions conducive to termite activity** — moisture, wood-to-soil contact, drainage issues, high-risk landscaping
5. **Risk rating** — an assessment of the overall termite risk for the property
6. **Recommendations** — specific treatment or remedial actions recommended based on the inspection findings

A termite inspection report is distinct from a **treatment certificate**, which is issued after treatment has been carried out and confirms what treatment was applied, what product was used, and the warranty period.

## When Is a Termite Inspection Report Required in Kenya?

### 1. Property Purchase (Pre-Sale Inspection)

When buying a property in Kenya — whether a house, apartment, commercial building, or industrial unit — a termite inspection report protects the buyer from purchasing a property with undisclosed termite damage. Most property sale agreements in Kenya do not include termite warranty clauses by default, which means once you take possession, all repair costs are your responsibility.

A termite inspection report before purchase:
- Confirms the presence or absence of active termite infestation
- Identifies existing structural damage that may affect the property's value or safety
- Provides grounds for price negotiation if infestation is found
- Protects the buyer from expensive surprises after occupation

### 2. Mortgage Applications

Multiple Kenyan banks and mortgage institutions require a current termite inspection report — or a treatment certificate from a licensed operator — before approving a mortgage or disbursing construction finance. Banks accept Pestraid Kenya's inspection reports and treatment certificates as part of the mortgage application documentation.

Banks that may require termite documentation include:
- Kenya Commercial Bank (KCB)
- Housing Finance Company (HFC)
- Stanbic Bank Kenya
- Absa Bank Kenya
- Co-operative Bank of Kenya (for real estate mortgage products)

Always confirm the specific document format required by your bank before commissioning the inspection.

### 3. Property Valuation for Insurance

Some property insurance policies in Kenya require evidence of a current pest inspection as part of the initial property valuation process. The inspection report demonstrates that the insured property is free from undisclosed defects (including termite damage) at the time the policy is taken out.

### 4. Defects Liability Claims

During the defects liability period of a property purchase, if termite damage is discovered and the seller had not disclosed a known infestation, the buyer may have grounds for a defects claim. A professional termite inspection report produced before or shortly after completion provides the evidence base for such a claim.

### 5. Annual Property Management

For commercial property managers, landlords with multiple residential units, and institutional property owners, annual termite inspection reports provide:
- A documented record of the property's condition over time
- Compliance evidence for insurance and regulatory requirements
- Early detection of infestation before costly damage occurs

## What a Professional Termite Inspection Report Contains

Pestraid Kenya's formal inspection reports are structured documents containing the following sections:

### Section 1: Property Details and Inspection Parameters

- Property address, type, and approximate age
- Name of inspector, PCPB licence number, and date of inspection
- Inspection scope: areas inspected and any areas that were inaccessible (with reasons)
- Limitations of the inspection (e.g., areas concealed by stored furniture, locked rooms, or built-in fixtures that were not dismantled)

### Section 2: Inspection Findings

**Subterranean Termite Activity:**
- Areas of active infestation confirmed (with description and photographs)
- Areas of historic infestation or past damage
- Active mud tubes: location, dimensions, and condition
- Structural elements affected: specific members identified, condition assessed

**Timber Condition Assessment:**
- Condition of all accessible structural and non-structural timber elements
- Assessment by area: roof space, wall frames, floor structure, external timber
- Condition rated as: Sound / Minor Damage / Moderate Damage / Severe Damage

**Conditions Conducive to Termite Attack:**
- Excessive moisture (identified using calibrated moisture meter with readings recorded)
- Wood-to-soil contact
- High-risk landscaping (mulch against building, vegetation overgrowth)
- Drainage deficiencies
- Concealed areas of high risk

### Section 3: Risk Assessment

An overall risk rating for the property on a scale (e.g., Low / Moderate / High / Very High) based on:
- Soil type and known regional termite pressure
- Property age and construction type
- Presence of existing infestation or conditions conducive to attack
- Current treatment status (treated with active warranty vs. untreated)

### Section 4: Photographs

Every significant finding is documented with a numbered photograph. Photograph captions reference the specific location within the property and describe what is shown.

### Section 5: Recommendations

Specific, actionable recommendations:
- **If infestation is found:** Specific treatment recommended (soil barrier, baiting, wood treatment), urgency level, and estimated treatment timeframe
- **If no infestation is found:** Preventive recommendations and suggested treatment type to establish a protective barrier
- **Remedial repair:** Identification of structural elements requiring repair by a builder or carpenter after treatment

### Section 6: Limitations and Disclaimers

The report clearly states:
- Concealed termite activity cannot be detected by any non-invasive inspection method
- Findings relate to conditions at the time of inspection only
- The report is valid for 30–90 days from the inspection date (banks typically require a report no older than 30–60 days)

## Termite Inspection Report vs Treatment Certificate: Key Differences

| Document | When Issued | What It Confirms |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection Report | After inspection (no treatment) | Current condition of the property |
| Treatment Certificate | After treatment is applied | What treatment was done, product used, warranty period |

Banks at the mortgage completion stage typically require a **treatment certificate** (confirming treatment has been applied). Banks at the pre-approval stage may accept an inspection report. Always confirm with your bank which document is needed and at what stage.

## How to Ensure Your Report Is Accepted by Kenyan Banks and Authorities

For a termite inspection report to be accepted by Kenyan banks, county councils, and insurance providers, it must be:

1. **Produced by a PCPB-licensed pest control operator** — the inspector's PCPB licence number must be stated on the report
2. **Dated within the validity period** — most banks require a report no older than 30–60 days
3. **Site-specific** — must include the property's physical address, description, and legal reference (title number or LR number)
4. **Signed and stamped** by the licensed company

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termite Inspection Reports

**How long is the inspection report valid?**
Most Kenyan banks accept reports that are no more than 30–60 days old. For property transactions, commission the inspection as close as possible to the signing of the sale agreement or mortgage application.

**Can I use an inspection report from one bank for a different bank?**
Generally yes, as long as the report is in the correct format and within the validity period. Some banks may have specific format requirements — confirm with your bank before the inspection.

**What happens if the report finds active termites?**
The bank or buyer can require that treatment be completed and a treatment certificate provided before the transaction proceeds. This is actually in the buyer's interest — treatment is negotiated as part of the sale rather than becoming the buyer's problem after purchase.

**Does the inspection cover all parts of the building?**
A comprehensive inspection covers all accessible areas. Some areas may be inaccessible (sealed crawl spaces, locked rooms, areas concealed by heavy furniture). The report explicitly states what was and was not inspected.

**Can I get a same-day report?**
In most cases, Pestraid Kenya can carry out the inspection and issue a draft report within 24 hours. Full printed, stamped reports with photographs typically take 1–2 business days.
