# Termite Treatment for Timber Structures in Kenya: Protecting Roofs, Frames and Timber Buildings

Kenya's construction industry uses timber extensively - in roof trusses, purlins, wall plates, window and door frames, fascia boards, pergolas, carports, staircases, and ceiling systems. In coastal Kenya and rural areas, entire buildings are timber-framed. In Nairobi's rapidly expanding suburbs, timber roof structures top almost every residential and commercial building. All of this timber is perpetually at risk from termite attack.

Termite treatment for timber structures is a specialized field that goes beyond standard soil barrier treatment. It involves understanding how different timber species respond to chemical treatment, how termites access above-ground timber elements, and how to protect structures that may have limited or no soil contact. This guide covers all the key aspects relevant to Kenyan homeowners, builders, architects, and property managers.

## How Termites Attack Timber Structures in Kenya

### Subterranean Access (Most Common in Kenya)

Kenya's dominant termite species - *Macrotermes* and *Coptotermes* - are subterranean. They live in soil colonies and reach above-ground timber by building **mud tubes** that travel up walls, columns, and structural members. These tubes protect the workers from desiccation and predators, allowing them to travel from soil level to roof trusses - a vertical journey of 6 metres or more - completely concealed.

The sequence of attack in a typical Kenyan residential building:
1. Colony established in soil beneath or adjacent to the building
2. Workers emerge from soil and build concealed mud tubes along foundation walls
3. Tubes extend up internal wall cavities, behind plasterboard, or up the external face of walls at night
4. Workers reach the wall plate (the horizontal timber that sits on top of the masonry and supports the roof trusses)
5. From the wall plate, workers spread into roof trusses, purlins, rafters, and ceiling battens
6. Feeding continues for months or years before any visible signs appear inside the occupied rooms below

### Drywood Termite Attack (Less Common but Serious)

Drywood termites (*Cryptotermes* species) establish colonies within the timber itself without soil contact. Colonies are typically smaller but can cause significant localized damage to high-value timber such as door frames, window sashes, and hardwood furniture. Signs include small round exit holes and pellet-shaped frass.

## Timber Elements at Highest Risk in Kenyan Buildings

Understanding which timber elements are most vulnerable helps prioritize inspection and treatment:

| Timber Element | Risk Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Roof trusses | Very High | Closest elevated timber to rising mud tubes from walls |
| Wall plate (top of masonry wall) | Very High | First timber element encountered by rising termites |
| Purlins and rafters | High | Directly connected to wall plate; frequently attacked |
| Ceiling battens | High | Provide pathway from wall plate across ceiling space |
| Door frames | High | Direct soil contact at bottom rail; common entry point |
| Window frames | Medium | Less frequent soil contact; wind-driven moisture increases risk |
| Staircase stringers and treads | Medium | Frequently in contact with floor structure |
| External pergolas, carports, decks | Very High | Timber posts in or near soil contact |
| Built-in furniture (wardrobes) | Medium | Fixed to walls; adjacent to treated areas |

## Treatment Methods for Timber Structures

### Method 1: Boron-Based Wood Preservative Treatment

Boron (typically in the form of disodium octaborate tetrahydrate, sold as Tim-Bor, Bora-Care, or equivalent) is the most widely used active ingredient for timber treatment in Kenya. Boron penetrates deeply into the wood's cell structure and:

- Kills termites that consume treated wood (boron disrupts their digestive microbiome)
- Kills termites on contact when they pass through treated timber surfaces
- Provides long-term protection to the treated timber (10+ years when protected from rain)
- Is low-toxicity to mammals and birds
- Does not affect the structural properties of timber

**Application method:** Boron solution is applied by brush, spray, or pressure injection to all exposed timber surfaces. For roof trusses and purlins, Pestraid Kenya's technicians work in the roof space, applying boron solution to all structural timber by brush or low-pressure spray.

**Best for:** Roof structures, ceiling systems, wall plates, new timber before installation, replacement timber after infestation.

### Method 2: Deltamethrin or Cypermethrin Contact Treatment

Pyrethroid-based insecticides (deltamethrin, cypermethrin) are applied to timber surfaces as a contact kill treatment. Workers that cross the treated surface receive a lethal dose. Less effective for colony elimination than the Transfer Effect of non-repellent soil treatments, but useful for rapid knockdown in active infestations.

**Best for:** Localized active infestations; supplementary treatment alongside soil barrier; external timber structures (pergolas, fence posts) where soil treatment is not practical.

### Method 3: Termiticide Foam Injection into Active Galleries

Where active termite galleries are confirmed within timber structural elements, Pestraid Kenya uses pressurized termiticide foam (e.g., Termidor Foam) injected through small drill holes. The foam expands to fill the gallery network, killing workers throughout the concealed colony pathway.

**Best for:** Confirmed active galleries within structural timber; wall cavities with confirmed activity; hollow column bases.

### Method 4: Soil Barrier Treatment (Primary Defence)

The most important protection for any timber structure in Kenya is the soil barrier treatment that prevents termites from leaving the ground and reaching the structure in the first place. All timber structure treatment programmes should include - or confirm the existence of - a current, warranted soil barrier treatment around the building's perimeter. Read our full guides on [Termite Soil Treatment in Kenya](/blog/termite-soil-treatment-kenya) and [Post Construction Termite Treatment](/blog/post-construction-termite-treatment-kenya).

Without a soil barrier, direct timber treatment alone is insufficient as a long-term solution. Termites will find new pathways to untreated sections of the structure.

## Treatment for Specific Timber Structure Types

### Roof Trusses and Structural Roof Timber

Pestraid Kenya's roof timber treatment programme:
1. Access inspection of the full roof space to map all timber elements and identify any existing infestation or damage
2. Application of boron-based preservative to all accessible structural timber surfaces
3. Application of termiticide foam to any identified active galleries
4. Inspection and sealing of all points where electrical wiring or pipes penetrate the roof structure (common termite access points)
5. Written condition report with recommendations for repair of any structurally compromised timber

### Timber-Frame Buildings

Full timber-frame buildings - found in coastal Kenya, rural areas, and some specialized residential projects in Nairobi - require both:
- **Comprehensive soil barrier treatment** around the full perimeter and beneath the floor structure
- **Full boron impregnation treatment** of all exposed structural timber before cladding is installed

For new timber-frame construction, boron treatment of all structural timber should be specified before any cladding or lining conceals the framing. Retro-treating concealed framing in an existing building requires drilling and injection, which is significantly more expensive.

### External Timber Structures: Pergolas, Decks, Carports

External structures are in direct or near-direct soil contact and are frequently attacked first. Treatment includes:
- **Post base treatment** - soil around and beneath each post base is treated with termiticide
- **Physical collar treatment** - installation of a physical barrier (stainless steel mesh collar or bituminous wrap) around each post at soil contact point
- **Full boron application** to all timber surfaces
- **Annual inspection** - critical for external structures that are less frequently inspected than interior rooms

### Timber Fencing and Boundary Posts

Timber fence posts set in Kenyan soil are typically attacked within 3–5 years without treatment. Preventive measures include:
- Use of naturally resistant timber species (Mvuli, Cedar, Cypress) for fence posts
- Boron pre-treatment of all posts before installation
- Use of concrete post shoes (metal brackets) to keep timber above soil level
- Annual inspection and retreatment as required

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I know if my roof trusses have termite damage?**
Book a professional inspection. Pestraid Kenya's technicians enter the roof space and assess all accessible structural timber using sounding techniques, moisture meters, and visual examination. Signs of damage include softened or hollow-sounding timber, frass deposits, and mud tube evidence.

**Can damaged roof trusses be repaired rather than replaced?**
This is a structural engineering question rather than a pest control question. Pestraid Kenya will document the extent of damage, and your structural engineer or builder will advise on whether repair or replacement is required. Treatment to eliminate the colony always precedes any structural repair.

**Is pre-treated timber available in Kenya?**
Boron-treated timber and CCA-treated timber are available from specialist timber suppliers in Kenya. Always confirm the treatment type and standard before purchasing timber for structural use.

**Can termites damage steel or concrete structures?**
No - termites cannot consume steel or concrete. However, they can exploit any timber within or attached to these structures, and can use hairline cracks or gaps in concrete structures as access highways to timber above.
