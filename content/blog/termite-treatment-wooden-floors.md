# Termite Treatment for Wooden Floors in Kenya: Save Your Parquet, Hardwood and Engineered Flooring

Wooden flooring is one of the most significant investments a Kenyan homeowner or commercial property owner can make. Whether it is a classical parquet floor in a Karen residence, solid hardwood in a Lavington townhouse, or engineered oak in a Westlands apartment, timber flooring adds enormous aesthetic and financial value to a property - and it is one of the most vulnerable elements to termite attack.

Kenya's subterranean termites consume timber from the inside out, feeding on the cellulose within the wood while leaving the surface veneer intact. This means a wooden floor can be extensively damaged internally while appearing completely normal from above.

By the time the floor starts to feel spongy, creak unexpectedly, or visibly buckle, the damage may already be structural. This guide covers everything you need to know about identifying, treating, and preventing termite damage to wooden floors in Kenya.

## Why Wooden Floors Are Such a High-Risk Zone for Termites

### Ground-Level Proximity

Most timber flooring in Kenya is installed either directly on or very close to the concrete sub-floor slab. This proximity to the ground means:

- **Moisture from the slab** creates the humid microclimate termites require
- **Any gap or penetration** in the slab or adhesive bed provides access to the underside of the flooring
- **The adhesive and underlay** used to fix flooring to the slab often contains organic materials that termites also consume

### Concealed Access Routes

Termites do not break through the floor surface. They build concealed mud galleries within the flooring system - between the slab and the underside of the timber boards - feeding systematically while remaining entirely invisible. This is why wooden floor infestations are frequently only discovered during a [professional termite inspection](/blog/termite-inspection-nairobi) using acoustic sounding equipment and moisture meters, not by visual examination.

### The Value of What Is Being Damaged

High-quality wooden flooring in Nairobi ranges from KES 3,000 to KES 12,000+ per square metre installed. For a 60m² living area, the flooring represents a KES 180,000–720,000 investment. The cost of replacing termite-damaged flooring - including the labour of removal, treatment, and reinstallation - can easily exceed the original installation cost.

## Signs of Termite Damage in Wooden Floors

Be alert for the following warning signs, which may indicate termite activity beneath or within your timber flooring:

**1. Spongy or springy areas** - sections of the floor that flex or bounce underfoot when they previously did not

**2. Hollow sound when tapping** - use a coin to tap across the floor surface; damaged sections produce a noticeably hollow sound compared to sound timber

**3. Buckling or lifting at joints** - parquet blocks or solid boards lifting at their edges or tongue-and-groove joints

**4. Fine cracks appearing in the floor surface** - particularly along board edges and at corners of parquet blocks

**5. Mud tubes visible beneath removable skirting boards** - check by removing skirting boards and examining the gap between the floor and the wall at the slab level

**6. Frass (termite excrement)** - very fine, sand-like powdery material appearing at the floor-to-wall junction or emerging from small pin-sized holes in the floor surface

**7. Discolouration or staining** - localized darkening of floor surface in specific patches, sometimes indicating moisture from termite activity beneath

If you observe any of these signs, do not attempt DIY treatment. Book a professional inspection immediately to establish the extent of the infestation before it progresses further.

## How Termites Get Into Wooden Floors: Entry Routes

Understanding how termites enter your floor helps in both treatment and prevention:

- **Sub-slab galleries** - termites travel in earthen galleries beneath the concrete sub-floor slab, emerging through micro-cracks, expansion joints, or the perimeter gap between the slab edge and the wall
- **Adhesive penetrations** - in glue-down floor installations, the adhesive is typically applied in a grid pattern leaving gaps that termites can exploit
- **Plumbing penetrations** - water pipes passing through the slab provide a termite highway from below-slab soil to the underside of the flooring
- **Wall-to-floor junction** - the gap behind skirting boards is a common entry and concealment route

## Termite Treatment Options for Existing Wooden Floor Infestations

### Treatment Option 1: Sub-Slab Injection Without Floor Removal

This is the preferred treatment approach where the floor is valuable and its removal would be prohibitively expensive. Pestraid Kenya applies termiticide to the soil beneath the slab:

1. **Small-diameter holes** (10–12mm) are drilled through the concrete sub-floor slab at the wall-to-floor junction, at plumbing penetrations, and at any identified expansion joints - targeting the key entry points
2. **Termiticide is injected** at pressure into the soil beneath, creating a treated zone that kills termites travelling from the soil toward the floor
3. **Drill holes are sealed** with concrete plugs, colour-matched where the holes are visible at the base of skirting boards

The wooden floor itself is not removed. Termites already present within the floor structure are eliminated through the Transfer Effect of the non-repellent termiticide: workers returning from the treated sub-slab zone carry Fipronil or Imidacloprid back to the colony, eventually eliminating it.

### Treatment Option 2: Floor Removal, Treatment, and Re-installation

For floors where the infestation is advanced and structural damage is extensive, the most effective approach involves:

1. **Removal of the damaged flooring** - carried out by a flooring specialist in coordination with Pestraid Kenya
2. **Full formation level treatment** - with the slab exposed, termiticide is applied directly to the sub-slab soil (equivalent to a post-construction sub-slab treatment as described in [Termite Soil Treatment in Kenya](/blog/termite-soil-treatment-kenya))
3. **Treatment of remaining timber elements** - any sub-floor timber battens or fixing strips are treated with a borate-based wood preservative
4. **Re-installation of flooring** - either the original boards (if undamaged) or replacement boards are installed by the flooring specialist

This approach provides the most complete barrier but requires the flooring cost to be replaced. For valuable timber floors, it is often the most cost-effective long-term solution.

### Treatment Option 3: Direct Wood Treatment (Injection into Galleries)

Where termites are confirmed within specific areas of the floor and the infestation is localized, Pestraid Kenya can inject termiticide foam or liquid directly into the termite galleries through small-diameter holes drilled through the floor or through the skirting board gap. This is a targeted supplementary method rather than a complete treatment.

### Treatment Option 4: Perimeter Barrier Treatment

As part of any floor treatment programme, Pestraid Kenya applies a full perimeter soil barrier treatment around the building's external foundation walls. This addresses the colony's access to the building as a whole, not just the floor in isolation. Without treating the perimeter, any floor treatment is incomplete.

## How to Prevent Termite Damage to New Wooden Floor Installations

If you are planning to install new wooden flooring, follow these preventive steps to minimize termite risk:

**1. Ensure sub-slab termite treatment is current.** Before installing any timber flooring, confirm that the building has a valid, in-warranty [termite soil treatment](/services/anti-termite-soil-treatment). If not, arrange treatment first and allow 30 days for the treatment to be fully effective before installation begins.

**2. Use borate-treated timber or pre-treated engineered boards.** Some premium engineered wood floor products are factory-treated with borate preservatives. Ask your supplier whether the product has been treated.

**3. Seal all sub-floor penetrations.** Before laying the floor, seal all visible gaps and penetrations in the concrete sub-floor slab with a flexible polyurethane sealant.

**4. Maintain an inspection gap.** Do not glue flooring boards right to the edge of the wall - allow the minimum expansion gap (typically 10mm) and ensure this gap is inspectable by not permanently sealing behind the skirting board.

**5. Schedule annual inspections.** Annual [termite inspections](/blog/termite-inspection-nairobi) detect sub-floor activity before it progresses to visible damage. The cost of an inspection is a fraction of the cost of replacing damaged flooring.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Termite Treatment for Wooden Floors

**Can my parquet floor be saved if termites are found?**
Often yes, if the damage is caught early. Parquet blocks that are hollow but structurally intact can be retained in many cases after treatment eliminates the colony.

Blocks that have lost structural integrity need replacement. A specialist inspection determines the extent of damage.

**How long does treatment take?**
Sub-slab injection treatment for a standard room can be completed in 2–4 hours. Full property treatment takes one full day.

**Do I need to remove furniture during treatment?**
Only in rooms where drilling through the slab is required. In adjacent rooms, furniture can remain in place. Pestraid Kenya's technicians will advise on specific preparation required.

**Can termites return to a treated floor?**
Once the colony is eliminated through the Transfer Effect of the termiticide, that specific colony cannot return. However, new colonies can establish over time as the chemical barrier depletes. Annual inspections and timely re-treatment at the end of the warranty period prevent recurrence.

**Is wood floor termite treatment safe for indoor air quality?**
Yes. Non-repellent termiticides applied to sub-slab soil have minimal above-slab vapour impact.

Drill holes are sealed immediately after treatment. Boron-based wood treatments applied to timber have zero odour and very low toxicity.
